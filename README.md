# DQN-Pong-Agent
In this project I built a Deep Q-Network from scratch in PyTorch and trained it to play Atari Pong. The agent takes raw pixel frames as input, stacks 4 frames together to capture motion, and learns to beat the built-in Atari AI through 2 million steps of experience.
The network uses three convolutional layers to process the visual input followed by two fully connected layers to output Q-values for each of the 6 possible actions. Training uses an epsilon-greedy policy that starts fully random and decays to 1% exploration over 500,000 steps, with a replay buffer of 150,000 transitions to break correlation between samples.
During training I ran into a critical memory leak that was causing RAM to grow by 7GB every 1,000 steps. After adding diagnostic logging I traced the issue to the zip(*transitions) call in the replay buffer sample method, which was unpacking the entire deque into a massive intermediate Python tuple every step faster than the garbage collector could release it. I fixed this by replacing it with direct NumPy array construction which eliminated the leak entirely and kept RAM flat throughout all 2 million steps.
The final agent achieved a mean reward of 8+ and individual game scores of 15-17 out of a possible 21 against the Atari AI, winning the majority of games. The built-in Pong AI is a simple rule-based tracker that moves toward the ball's current position at a fixed speed — the DQN learned to exploit this by finding angles and shot placements that consistently beat the fixed-speed opponent without ever being told what those weaknesses were.
